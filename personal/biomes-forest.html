Forest is the second Biomes world, continuing the study Desert started. A river runs through an old northern forest. A yellow twin-prop plane has crashed in the shallows, and upstream a giant stone head pours water from its mouth into a walled basin, which spills over a ledge as a waterfall. There are no characters this time: the subject is the environment, the water and the atmosphere.

The goal was a world that holds up as a still and moves well: wind in the trees, water that flows, and foam that sits where real foam would. With the models coming from Tripo3D, most of my time went into the production side: water bodies carved into the landscape, effects fitted to the real heights in the level, a procedural forest, backlit light and fog, and a render that matches what I saw in the editor.

What I built

Brought the hero models in from Tripo3D (the stone temple head, the ruin arch, the wall segments, the crashed plane and the river terrain) and set them up in Unreal: scale, placement, materials, and collision on the stairs so you can walk them.
Built and sculpted the landscape behind the river (the raised terrace, the ground under the temple and the back forest), and set up Unreal Water bodies to carve into it: the main river runs down the terrain, and a second river body carves the walled basin's bed into the landscape.
Shaped the Niagara effects for this scene, tuned to read well on camera and stay light on performance, and fitted to the level's real heights: the ledge waterfall running wall to wall, the thick mouth pour onto the temple platform, foam around the rocks and the plane, a foam trail that drifts downstream and stays inside the banks, and engine smoke from the plane.
Made the foam follow shapes around the arch and the plane hull. Hidden proxy meshes trace each outline, and GPU mesh sampling spawns the foam along them.
Scattered the back forest with PCG on a sculpted landscape in four layers (conifers, mid trees, saplings, undergrowth), with exclusion zones keeping the water and the temple clear.
Dressed the river with Megaplants trees on dynamic wind, undergrowth and Megascans rocks and cliffs, and used Megascans cliffs again for the distant mountains.
Lighting: a backlit sun, volumetric fog and god rays, and aerial perspective that puts a blue haze on the mountains and a light depth wash over the back forest.
Ambient sound from ElevenLabs sound effects (river, waterfall, wind), placed in the level and recorded straight from the engine with the render.
Walkable: a third-person character can walk the banks and the temple stairs.

Tech notes

The new river system didn't make it. I first tried Unreal's new Shallow Water River (Water Advanced) for flow and foam around the rocks, but its simulation baked out empty, even in a clean 5.8.3 test project with a landscape, and the likely fix (collision profiles) turned out to be a dead end. So I rolled back to the standard Water Body River and built the foam and ripples around the rocks and the plane with Niagara instead.
Two water bodies hiding the foam. The basin had two overlapping river bodies, and one of them was hidden. The hidden one still set the water height, so foam placed right at the surface rendered underneath it. I found it by raising a test actor in steps until it appeared, then put the foam about 14 cm above the surface it was supposed to sit on.
A plant-wind gizmo showing up in the render. The Megaplants wind controller has real mesh icons. They look like editor-only helpers but still show in Movie Render Queue. I hid them in game, and the wind still works.
Measure the drop, don't trust the note. The waterfall was tuned for the wrong height. Reading the actual water-surface heights put the fall at about 5.7 m, so I re-timed the particle lifetimes to end the sheet at the waterline instead of letting it sink through.

The journey

Forest reused the render side Desert built: shared Movie Render Queue presets, one sequence per camera, and beauty, lighting and wireframe passes rendered headless through Darkroom. That left this world free to be about water and environment. The walled basin is a second Water body that carves its own bed into a sculpted landscape. The back forest is PCG on that same landscape, which only placed trees once the landscape's collision was working.

What's next

A new temple. First up, I want to redesign the stone temple head with a stronger, more detailed design, so the centrepiece holds up in closer shots.
Wildlife. I plan to bring back the original "hunt" idea for this world, with animals moving through the forest and along the river.
A richer water pass. I want the shoreline foam and flow to come from the water system itself, replacing the Niagara foam collars I placed by hand.
Time of day and weather. I'll add changing light and weather, like rain or mist rolling down the valley, to see how the same world reads in a different mood.
